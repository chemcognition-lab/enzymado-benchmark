> Binary prediction of whether a protein binds a given small-molecule ligand, from the sequence and ligand SMILES.

> Benchmark-card description: Protein-ligand binding / annotation-derived binding labels.

## Source & provenance

- **Source dataset:** BioLiP (benchmark provenance: `BioLiP`)

A 0/1 label for whether an enzyme–ligand pair is a binder. Positives come from BioLiP's biologically relevant ligand–protein interactions; negatives are generated by pairing proteins with ligands they are not annotated to bind.

**Assay / measurement:** Structure-derived binding annotations from BioLiP (ligands observed in contact with protein residues in experimental structures), filtered to enzymes and to drug-like ligands.

**Provenance & construction:** Cleaned from the BioLiP non-redundant release: enzymes only (non-null EC), ligand IDs mapped to SMILES via the PDB Chemical Component Dictionary, monoatomic ions and polymers removed, then one negative sampled per positive (seed 42, deduplicated on sequence+SMILES).

## Featurization

The protein embedding is concatenated with an 8-dim RDKit descriptor vector computed from the ligand SMILES (MolWt, MolLogP, TPSA, HBD, HBA, rotatable bonds, rings, heavy atoms).

## When to use

A protein+ligand classification task probing recognition/binding — useful for virtual screening and substrate-scope questions.

## Caveats

Negatives are synthetic (unannotated pairs may still bind weakly), and BioLiP structural coverage biases the positive set. Ligands use the simple 8-dim RDKit featurization.
