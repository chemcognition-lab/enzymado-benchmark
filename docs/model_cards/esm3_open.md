## 0. How to think about this model

**ESM3 1.4B Open** (`esm3_open`, `EvolutionaryScale/esm3-sm-open-v1`, loaded via `ESM3.from_pretrained("esm3_sm_open_v1")`) is a 48-layer, 1,536-dimensional bidirectional multimodal generative Transformer (`1.4B` / `1,400M` parameters) trained with a variable-rate generative masked language modeling objective across **seven discrete protein tracks**: (a) amino-acid sequence, (b) 3D backbone atomic coordinates (processed via an $SE(3)$-invariant Geometric Attention sub-layer in the first block only), (c) local 3D structure tokens from a 4,096-codebook VQ-VAE, (d) 8-class secondary structure (`SS8`), (e) 16-bin solvent-accessible surface area (`SASA`), (f) LSH-quantized InterPro/GO function keyword tokens, and (g) 1,478-dimensional multi-hot InterPro residue annotations. Unlike the 50K-step compute-optimal scaling model (`1.4B` trained on ~80B tokens), `ESM3-open` is intentionally **overtrained 4-fold for 200K steps (~300B–320B tokens, $2.7 \times 10^{21}$ FLOPs)** to maximize inference and representation quality for the academic research community, while excluding Observed Antibody Space (OAS) sequences and filtering out **10.6M viral/select-agent sequences** and **9,462 viral/toxin keywords**. Crucially, in Enzymado's embedding pipeline (`embedding_scripts/esm3_embedding_script.py`), `esm3_open` is queried in **sequence-only mode** (`ESMProtein(sequence=seq)`), leaving all structural and functional input tracks unpopulated/masked so that downstream probes rely on the multimodal structure-and-function priors internalized into the shared 1,536-D latent space during pretraining.

- **Best-suited dataset regimes:**
  - 3D structure- and active-site-sensitive enzyme tasks (catalytic site and binding-site residue annotation, fold-conditioned kinetic or stability regression) where joint pretraining to reconstruct 4,096 VQ-VAE local neighborhood tokens, `SASA`, `SS8`, and 1,478 InterPro residue annotations shapes the final hidden representation.
  - Multi-domain enzymes up to 2,048 residues, which lie strictly within ESM3-open's native 2,048-token pretraining context window.
  - Broad non-viral prokaryotic and eukaryotic enzyme superfamilies covered by UniRef, MGnify, JGI, PDB, AlphaFoldDB, and ESMAtlas (including 362M synthetic sequences generated by a 200M inverse-folding model).
- **Expected vulnerabilities & blind spots:**
  - **Sequence-only inference under-specification in Enzymado:** Enzymado calls `ESMProtein(sequence=seq)` with only the 1D sequence track populated; structure coordinates, structure tokens, `SS8`, `SASA`, function tokens, and InterPro residue annotations are left unpopulated (mapped to mask/zero vectors, with first-layer Geometric Attention inactive). Although whole-track dropout during pretraining (`0.25` to `0.90`) trains ESM3 to operate when non-sequence tracks are absent, sequence-only extraction does not leverage explicit 3D coordinate or functional prompting.
  - **Biosecurity and antibody filtering blind spots:** `ESM3-open` omits all OAS antibody sequences, removes 10.6M sequences aligning to viral or CDC/USDA Select Agent & Toxin denylists across PDB, UniRef, MGnify, and JGI, and strips 9,462 virus/toxin keywords (`14%` of the 68,103-keyword vocabulary; `[SI, p. 64]`).
  - **Generative noise-schedule trade-off:** Whereas encoder-only PLMs (ESM-2, ESMC) dedicate 100% of updates to a fixed 15% mask rate, ESM3 samples sequence masking from `betalinear30` (80% $\beta(3,9)$ + 20% uniform up to 100% masking, mean 30%; `[SI, p. 39]`) to support iterative unconditional generation.
- **Downstream probe interaction summary:** Enzymado extracts `output.per_residue_embedding[1:-1]` (equivalently `out.embeddings[:, 1 : slen + 1, :]`), the final 48th-layer fused representation ($\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$) that directly feeds six parallel 2-layer GeLU regression heads predicting sequence, structure tokens, `SS8`, `SASA`, function keywords, and InterPro residue annotations. Despite having more than twice the parameters of `esm2_650M` (`1,400M` vs. `650M`), its deep 48-layer design keeps embedding width moderate ($D = 1{,}536$, only 20% wider than `esm2_650M` and 40% narrower than `esm2_3B`), keeping both mean-pooled (`linear`, `mlp`, `xgboost`) and per-residue attention probes (`attention_linear`, `attention_mlp`) well-conditioned.

## 1. Representational mechanisms & dataset compatibility guide

### 1.1 Objective & latent geometry
- **Mechanism:** All seven input modalities are embedded into $\mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$ and summed at the input of the Transformer trunk (`z_seq + z_plddt + z_structure + z_ss8 + z_sasa + z_func + z_res`; `[SI, p. 23, Algorithm 3]`), while 3D backbone frames $T \in SE(3)^L$ are processed by an $SE(3)$-invariant Geometric Attention sub-layer in Block 1 only (`[SI, p. 23, Algorithm 2]`). At the top of the 48-layer stack, shallow 2-layer GeLU MLP regression heads (`proj_in -> GeLU -> LayerNorm -> proj_out`; `[SI, p. 24, Algorithm 4]`) project the shared final representation into per-track logits under a generative masked language modeling objective. To balance representation learning with iterative generation, sequence corruption uses `betalinear30` (80% $\beta(3,9)$ with mean 25% + 20% uniform, overall mean mask rate 30%), while non-sequence tracks undergo both continuous noise schedules and whole-track dropout (`0.25` for structure tokens, `0.50` for coordinates, `0.90` for `SS8`, `SASA`, function, and residue annotations; `[SI, pp. 39, 41, Table S6]`).
  > *"ESM3 is a bidirectional transformer. Sequence, structure, and function tokens are embedded and fused at the input and then processed through a stack of transformer blocks (Fig. 1B). At the output of the model, shallow multilayer perceptron heads project the final layer representation into token probabilities for each of the tracks."* `[Main, p. 1]`
  > *"80% of the time, the mask rate is sampled from a β(3, 9) distribution with mean mask rate 25%. 20% of the time, the mask rate is sampled from a uniform distribution, resulting in an average overall mask rate of 30%."* `[SI, p. 39]`
  > *"Along with applying noise to each track, we want to ensure ESM3 is able to perform well when some tracks are not provided at all (e.g. to perform structure prediction when no structure is provided as input). We enable this by wholly dropping out some tracks with varying probabilities, listed in Table S6."* `[SI, p. 39]`
- **Downstream task implications:** Because the final 48th-layer representation must simultaneously predict 4,096 VQ-VAE local 3D structure tokens, `SS8`, `SASA`, LSH function tokens, and 1,478 InterPro residue features through 2-layer GeLU heads even when all non-sequence tracks are dropped out at input, sequence-only embeddings carry explicitly supervised 3D structural and catalytic-site geometry.

### 1.2 Pretraining corpus filtering & 4x overtraining of `ESM3-open`
- **Mechanism:** The full ESM3 corpus comprises 3.15 billion sequences, 236 million structures (PDB, AlphaFoldDB v4, ESMAtlas), 362 million synthetic sequences inverse-folded from AFDB and ESMAtlas structures, and 539 million InterPro/GO-annotated proteins (`771B` unique tokens; `[Main, p. 3]`, `[SI, p. 40, Tables S3–S4]`). To maximize open-model inference quality, `ESM3-open` is overtrained 4-fold for `200K` steps (`~300B–320B` tokens vs. `50K` steps / `~80B` tokens for the scaling baseline; `[SI, pp. 24, 42, 44]`), while omitting OAS antibodies and removing 10.6M viral/select-agent sequences and 9,462 viral/toxin keywords (`[SI, p. 64]`).
  > *"Overall, this increases training data to 3.15 billion protein sequences, 236 million protein structures, and 539 million proteins with function annotations, totaling 771 billion unique tokens."* `[Main, p. 3]`
  > *"To enable efficient inference for the benefit of the research community, we have trained two additional versions of ESM3 1.4B, named 1.4B Overtrained and 1.4B Open, which are trained on 300B tokens, far beyond their compute optimality for training."* `[SI, p. 42]`
  > *"ESM3-open is a 1.4B-parameter model we trained without OAS antibody sequences and with precautionary risk mitigations for release to the academic research community."* `[SI, p. 64]`
  > *"For each denylist, MMseqs was used to query against the full set of training databases, (including PDB, UniRef, MGnify, and JGI) and all hits were removed from the training set. This filter removes a total of 10.6M sequences across all training sets."* `[SI, p. 64]`
- **Downstream task implications:** Four-fold overtraining substantially improves single-sequence structural representations (boosting CAMEO single-pass argmax LDDT from `0.775` to `0.805` and iterative LDDT from `0.807` to `0.830`; `[SI, p. 44, Table S8]`), while the denylist filtering slightly reduces LDDT relative to the un-filtered `1.4B Overtrained` checkpoint (`0.830` vs. `0.846`) and creates an intentional blind spot on viral and select-agent proteins.

### 1.3 Training crop window & positional encoding
- **Mechanism:** Uses Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) across all 48 layers and trains natively at a context length of 2,048 tokens (`[SI, pp. 23–24, Table S1]`), augmented with random chainbreak/residue-gap splitting (`[SI, p. 39]`).
  > *"We use Pre-LN instead of Post-LN (62), rotary embeddings (65) instead of absolute positional embeddings, and we replace ReLU non-linearity with SwiGLU (66)."* `[SI, p. 23]`
  > *"1.4B 48 1536 64 2048 4.0e-4 5K 1,572,864 200K ∼320B 2.7 × 1021"* `[SI, p. 24, Table S1]`
- **Downstream task implications:** Like ESMC, `esm3_open` natively accommodates proteins up to 2,048 residues without out-of-distribution RoPE extrapolation.

### 1.4 Depth-favored scaling, width $D = 1{,}536$ & probe parameterization
- **Mechanism:** Based on empirical ablations showing higher representation-learning returns from depth than width, `esm3_open` pairs a 48-layer Pre-LN Transformer trunk with a moderate hidden dimension $D = 1{,}536$ ($H = 24$ heads of dimension 64, bias-free SwiGLU FFN of width $4{,}096 = \frac{8}{3}D$), stabilized by QK-LayerNorm and residual scaling $s = 36 / n_{\text{layers}} = 0.75$ (`[Main, p. 3]`, `[SI, pp. 23–24, 41]`).
  > *"In an initial series of experiments to evaluate representation learning performance in response to architecture hyperparameters, we found a greater response to increasing depth than to width."* `[Main, p. 3]`
  > *"ESM3-small (1.4B) is a 48-layer network, while ESM3-medium (7B) has 96 layers, and ESM3-large (98B) has 216 layers."* `[SI, p. 23]`
- **Downstream task implications:** Concentrating 1.4B parameters into 48 layers rather than a wide hidden state keeps the extracted representation at $D = 1{,}536$—40% narrower than `esm2_3B` ($D = 2{,}560$)—preventing parameter bloat in downstream linear, MLP, XGBoost, and attention-pooling probes.

### 3.2 Extracted Scientific Claims
- **`[esm3-c1 · multimodal_architecture]`** ESM3 represents sequence, 3D structure, and biological function as discrete token tracks fused into a single latent space within an all-to-all bidirectional Transformer trained with a variable-rate masked generative objective.
  > *"In ESM3, sequence, structure, and function are represented through alphabets of discrete tokens. The modalities are input and output as separate sequence tracks that are fused into a single latent space within the model."* `[Main, p. 1]`
- **`[esm3-c2 · representation_efficiency]`** On CAMEO contact prediction (LoRA + MLP head), the 50K-step ESM3 1.4B model achieves `P@L = 0.76 ± 0.02`, surpassing the 3B-parameter sequence-only ESM-2 model (`0.75 ± 0.02`) while using 27-fold less pretraining compute ($6.72 \times 10^{20}$ vs. $1.8 \times 10^{22}$ FLOPs).
  > *"The smallest ESM3 model, with 1.4B parameters, achieves a P@L of 0.76 ± 0.02 on the CAMEO test set, which is higher than the 3B parameter ESM2 model (0.75 ± 0.02). Furthermore, it trains on an order of magnitude less compute during pretraining (6.72 × 1020 FLOPs vs. 1.8 × 1022 FLOPs), demonstrating the benefits of multimodal pretraining."* `[SI, p. 42]`
- **`[esm3-c3 · overtraining_and_open_release]`** Overtraining ESM3 1.4B four-fold from 50K steps to 200K steps in `1.4B Open` increases single-sequence CAMEO structure prediction LDDT from `0.807` to `0.830` (iterative $O(L^3)$) and from `0.775` to `0.805` (single-pass argmax $O(L^2)$).
  > *"1.4B Open 0.830 0.705 0.733 0.805 0.640 0.677"* `[SI, p. 44, Table S8]`
- **`[esm3-c4 · architecture_depth_vs_width]`** In hyperparameter scaling experiments on representation learning, deeper networks yielded stronger returns than wider networks, motivating the 48-, 96-, and 216-layer architectures.
  > *"In an initial series of experiments to evaluate representation learning performance in response to architecture hyperparameters, we found a greater response to increasing depth than to width."* `[Main, p. 3]`
- **`[esm3-c5 · noise_schedule_tradeoff]`** A fixed 15% MLM mask rate gave poor generative capability whereas a uniform linear mask schedule degraded representation learning; the `betalinear30` mixture (80% $\beta(3,9)$ + 20% uniform, average 30% mask rate) balanced both objectives.
  > *"In initial experimentation, we found that a fixed 15% noise schedule led to poor generation results, while a linear noise schedule where probability of each mask rate was constant led to good generation but poor representation learning results."* `[SI, p. 39]`
- **`[esm3-c6 · programmable_design_and_gfp]`** Prompting ESM3 with active-site coordination constraints and chain-of-thought sampling generated `esmGFP`, a functional fluorescent protein at 58% sequence identity (96 mutations) to the closest known fluorescent protein.
  > *"Among the generations that we synthesized, we found a bright fluorescent protein at a far distance (58% sequence identity) from known fluorescent proteins, which we estimate is equivalent to simulating 500 million years of evolution."* `[Main, p. 1]`

## 4. Paper-Reported Limitations, Biases & Caveats

1. **Deliberate omission of OAS antibodies and removal of 10.6M viral / select-agent sequences in `ESM3-open`:** To mitigate biosecurity risks in the open-weights release, `ESM3-open` was trained without OAS antibodies and after removing all MMseqs2 hits against a Viral Denylist (~4M viral UniProt sequences) and Select Agents & Toxins Denylist (147K non-viral + 40K viral sequences), totaling 10.6M removed sequences across PDB, UniRef, MGnify, and JGI.
   > *"ESM3-open is a 1.4B-parameter model we trained without OAS antibody sequences and with precautionary risk mitigations for release to the academic research community."* `[SI, p. 64]`
   > *"For each denylist, MMseqs was used to query against the full set of training databases, (including PDB, UniRef, MGnify, and JGI) and all hits were removed from the training set. This filter removes a total of 10.6M sequences across all training sets."* `[SI, p. 64]`
2. **Removal of 9,462 virus- and toxin-related function keywords:** 14% of the original 68,103 TF-IDF function keywords were removed from `ESM3-open`'s LSH function vocabulary.
   > *"Of the original 68,103 keywords that ESM3 is trained with, this filter removes a total of 9,462 (14%), creating a new vocabulary of 58,641 keywords."* `[SI, p. 64]`
3. **Modest structure-prediction gap relative to un-filtered `1.4B Overtrained`:** At the same 200K training steps, `1.4B Open` scores slightly lower than `1.4B Overtrained` on CAMEO (`0.830` vs. `0.846` iterative LDDT; `0.805` vs. `0.825` argmax LDDT) and CASP15 (`0.733` vs. `0.750` iterative LDDT; `[SI, p. 44, Table S8]`).
4. **Mode switching under function keyword prompting:** Under keyword prompting, a fraction of generated proteins fold into high-confidence structures that fail to recover the prompted function keywords (`[Main, p. 4]`).
   > *"Some mode switching is observed, including under key word prompting, where a fraction of the generations have confidently predicted structures that do not recover the key words."* `[Main, p. 4]`

## 5. Enzymado Embedding & Probing Specification

*(Status: `code` — deterministic pipeline from `SUPPORTING_CONTEXT_MODELS.md` and `embedding_scripts/esm3_embedding_script.py`)*

- **Enzymado Key & Checkpoint:** `esm3_open` $\rightarrow$ `EvolutionaryScale/esm3-sm-open-v1` (loaded via `ESM3.from_pretrained("esm3_sm_open_v1")`, `1,400M` registry parameters, $D = 1{,}536$).
- **Model Loader:** `esm.models.esm3.ESM3.from_pretrained("esm3_sm_open_v1", device=device)`.
- **Sequence-Only Input Mode & Tokenization:** Gap characters (`-`) are stripped during FASTA loading. Each protein is instantiated via `protein = ESMProtein(sequence=seq)` with **only the 1D amino-acid sequence track populated** (3D structure coordinates, VQ-VAE structure tokens, `SS8`, `SASA`, function keyword tokens, and InterPro residue annotations are left unpopulated/masked). The protein is encoded via `protein_tensor = client.encode(protein)` and passed through `output = client.forward_and_sample(protein_tensor, SamplingConfig(return_per_residue_embeddings=True))`.
- **Special-Token Slicing:** ESM3 prepends BOS at index `0` and appends EOS at index `-1`; Enzymado slices `emb = output.per_residue_embedding[1:-1]` (equivalently `out.embeddings[:, 1 : slen + 1, :]`) from the final (48th) Transformer layer so that strictly the $L$ amino-acid residue representations $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$ are retained.
- **Storage Format:** Saved in `bfloat16` (`BF16`) safetensors shards both as per-residue matrices $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$ (`SafetensorsShardWriter`) and as mean-pooled sequence vectors $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prot}} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^L \mathbf{E}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$ (`SafetensorsPooledWriter`).
- **Downstream Probing Heads Evaluated (5 heads across 140 runs per PLM):**
  1. `linear` (`pooling="no_attention"`, `head="linear"`): Ridge / L2-regularized linear or logistic/SGD probe over mean-pooled $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$.
  2. `mlp` (`pooling="no_attention"`, `head="mlp"`): 2-layer feed-forward neural network with normalization, activation, and dropout over mean-pooled $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$.
  3. `xgboost` (`pooling="no_attention"`, `head="xgboost"`): Gradient-boosted decision trees (`XGBRegressor` / `XGBClassifier`) over mean-pooled $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$.
  4. `attention_linear` (`pooling="attention"`, `head="linear"`): Learned per-residue attention pooling (`ResidualAttentionAggregation` for global tasks; `AttentionResidueMixer` for residue tasks) trained jointly with a linear readout head over $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$.
  5. `attention_mlp` (`pooling="attention"`, `head="mlp"`): Learned per-residue attention pooling trained jointly with a 2-layer MLP readout head over $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$.
- **Task-Layout Conditioning:**
  - **Layout A (`protein_global`):** Uses $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$ (concatenated with standardized reaction conditions such as temperature, pH, and buffer one-hot indicators when present).
  - **Layout B (`protein_ligand_global`):** Concatenates $\mathbf{e}_{\text{prot}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$ with 200 normalized RDKit 2D substrate descriptors (`descriptastorus` `RDKit2DNormalized`).
  - **Layout C (`mutation_delta_global`):** Uses the wild-type subtraction vector $\mathbf{e}_{\text{mut}} - \mathbf{e}_{\text{wt}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$ for point-mutation thermostability ($\Delta T_m$, $\Delta\Delta G$) tasks.
  - **Layout D (`protein_residue`):** Predicts per-residue binary labels $\{0, 1\}^L$ directly from $\mathbf{E} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 1536}$.
